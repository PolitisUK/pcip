Participant account
"We were told that the more reports the council received, the more likely it was that something would be done. My neighbours and I reported the same problems almost every day for months, but nothing changed. Eventually we stopped reporting them. Why the platform exists

Administrative data often shows what happened. Qualitative research helps explain why.

Administrative data and quantitative approaches can show scale, frequency and patterns. Consultation has democratic and, where applicable, statutory value. These sources do not always explain why people behave as they do, who is absent from the record, or how a policy is experienced in everyday life.

Scale without explanation

Numbers can identify a pattern. They do not always explain what may be driving it.

A fall in reports may look like improvement. Qualitative enquiry may show that residents have stopped reporting because they no longer believe it will make a difference.

Consultation in context

Consultation matters, but it need not carry the whole evidence base.

Ongoing enquiry can test assumptions before formal consultation begins and provide context for interpreting the responses.

The people who are missing

The usual respondents cannot speak for everyone affected.

Purposive recruitment can bring seldom-heard perspectives into the record. It provides depth and context, not statistical representation.

Citizen Centric does not replace surveys, consultation or service data. It helps teams use them as part of a broader and more accountable evidence base.

An iterative research process

Build understanding before the answer is fixed.

The platform keeps the research enquiry, participant relationship, consent, research material and communication connected as the study develops.

01

Build understanding

Start with a place, service, community or emerging signal, including where the issue is not yet clearly defined. Use early research to surface experiences, patterns and questions that need closer attention.

02

Design the research

Choose methods and sampling deliberately for what you are trying to understand. Combine qualitative and quantitative approaches where appropriate, with a clear rationale for how different forms of evidence will inform one another.

03

Develop evidence over time

Gather participant accounts, observations, media and structured measures as the study develops. Follow emerging themes, test interpretations and adapt the enquiry where the evidence points to something that needs further exploration.

04

Inform decisions and keep learning

Interpret findings alongside other evidence to improve services, support governance and strengthen representations to other organisations. Feed what is learned back into the next cycle of research rather than treating the study as a one-off exercise.

Research is iterative. What is learned can reshape the enquiry, methods and the next stage of evidence gathering.

What makes Citizen Centric different

Depth, structure and continuity in one research environment.

Citizen Centric is not a survey builder or a generic feedback channel. It gives Place councils a structured way to understand context, follow experience over time and connect different forms of material with the continuity and traceability that support the development of decision-grade evidence.

  • Qualitative depth. Use qualitative methods, including diaries, reflection, media and follow-up communication, to examine everyday experience, context, routines, barriers and workarounds.
  • Mixed-methods integration. Design studies so qualitative accounts and structured measures inform one another during analysis and interpretation.
  • Community-based enquiry. Give researchers and trained community ethnographers a consistent process for collecting contributions responsibly.
  • Continuity. Build a body of insight across time instead of relying only on a one-off exercise.

Trust and research governance

A credible finding depends on a credible process.

Citizen Centric gives authorised teams practical controls for managing access, consent, files, communication and participant rights within a defined study.

Scoped access

Role-based researcher permissions and study-specific participant sessions keep access tied to the work people are responsible for.

Consent and status

Consent, enrolment and participation states remain visible to the research team as the study progresses.

Evidence handling

File validation, malware-screening states and protected downloads support safer handling of participant-provided material.

Privacy workflows

Export, deletion, anonymisation and withdrawal workflows help teams respond consistently to participant requests.

Research material becomes evidence through an appropriate, transparent process. Findings depend on sound research design, appropriate sampling, suitable methods, careful interpretation and professional judgement. The platform supports that work. It does not replace it.

From insight to action

Use stronger evidence in two directions.

Research findings can improve decisions within a Place council. They can also support a clearer, better-supported case where a principal authority, government body, regulator or partner holds the decision.

Decisions you are responsible for

Improve policy, services and organisational learning.

Use findings to define the right problem, understand how a Place council service works in practice and work with residents to develop and test possible options before committing to a solution.

  • Parks, open spaces, community buildings and local services
  • Policy development and review
  • Evidence-led co-design
  • Learning, governance and transparent decision records
Decisions made by others

Influence change with more than assertion or anecdote.

Where another organisation holds the statutory function, Citizen Centric helps Place councils prepare a structured account of local experience. It strengthens the representation. It does not guarantee that the decision will change.

  • Consultee responses, planning representations and planning enforcement concerns
  • Statutory, policy and service consultations
  • Evidence submitted to principal authorities, government and regulators
  • Briefings for commissioners and partner organisations
  • Advocacy and collaboration grounded in accountable research
